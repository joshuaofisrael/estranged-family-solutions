Sherry Ilyse Wexler, LMSW

Clinical social worker and therapist

Sherry Ilyse Wexler, LMSW, is a Michigan clinical social worker and a co-founder of Estranged Family Solutions with Marcie Israel. She has been in clinical practice since 1988. Her path in clinical social work includes Wayne State University School of Medicine. She was formerly known as Sherry Sack.

People who work with her can expect a collaborative alliance: attention to the connection between them, and help responding when an obstacle makes change feel out of reach. The aim is a more livable peace. With Marcie, she co-leads this practice’s care for family estrangement — repair when a relationship can be made safe, and support for a firm distance when it cannot.

Her wider clinical work includes anxiety, depression, OCD, ADHD, grief, body-focused repetitive behaviors, PTSD, neurodivergence, life transitions, questions of identity, and aging. Gifted children and adults are a particular passion of hers. She brings that range to the estrangement work she co-leads here.

What this practice is

Adults come here for repair and reunite, when a healthier relationship may be possible, or safe distance and coping, when it is not. That question organizes the practice.

The practice serves West Bloomfield, Farmington Hills, and Metro Detroit. Sherry meets clients by telehealth on weekdays, and on occasional weekends. This site does not publish an insurance panel or a fee schedule. Ask about current availability when you get in touch.

How the work is bounded

  • We do not promise a reunion, and we do not treat distance as a moral failure.
  • We will not help you pressure, monitor, or punish a relative who has asked for space.
  • We will not tell you that staying is always braver, or that leaving is always the cure.
  • Coming to therapy is not the same as bringing your whole family into the room. If more than one person wants to attend, ask. This site does not describe a joint-session or mediation program.

Sessions are for the adult who comes in. Parents are welcome as clients. This practice is not described here as therapy for children.
