Care path

Repair and reunite, when a healthier relationship is possible.

This path is for estranged family members who want to explore reconnection — carefully, and only where it can happen without asking anyone to return to harm.

Who this path is for

  • You want some form of relationship again, and you are willing to look at your own part in how it broke.
  • You are a parent hoping to understand an adult child’s distance, and to approach them without pressure.
  • You are an adult child who misses a parent, sibling, or other relative and wants to test whether contact can be different.
  • Contact already exists, but it is brittle. Holidays, logistics, or one repeated argument keep reopening the same injury.

Longing is not the same as readiness. You can love someone and still be right that the old arrangement was not safe. This path treats that distinction as the work, not as resistance.

What the work looks like

Therapy here is a conversation with a clear aim. It is not a promise that time, by itself, will soften a family. With Sherry Wexler or Marcie Israel, you can:

  • Say what “repair” would mean in concrete terms — a phone call, a narrower relationship, an apology that names something specific — rather than a return to how things used to be.
  • Practice what you want to say, and what you are actually able to hear, before you say it to your family.
  • Look at patterns that made closeness unsafe or exhausting: criticism, role reversal, silence used as punishment, loyalty conflicts.
  • Plan a pace for contact that either person can pause.

When reunification may be appropriate

Reconnection is worth considering when the people involved can tolerate limits, when past harm can be spoken about without immediate retaliation, and when no one is being asked to manage another adult’s emotions as the price of peace.

A workable repair is usually smaller than the hope that brought someone in. It might be a relationship with fewer topics, less time together, and a clearer right to step out of a conversation. Smaller is not a failure if it is honest.

When it may not be

Reunification is not the goal when contact would put someone back in danger, when one person refuses any limit, or when “repair” is being used to mean “agree that nothing serious happened.”

This practice will not help you wear a relative down, show up uninvited, monitor their life, or recruit other family members to apply pressure. If safety is the central issue, safe distance and coping is the more honest path.

You can change your mind

A wish to reconnect does not lock you into contact. A period of distance does not disqualify you from trying again later, if the conditions change. Many people use a first stretch of therapy to find out which path they are actually on.
